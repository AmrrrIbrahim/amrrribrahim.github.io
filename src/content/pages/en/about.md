## Who I am

I'm a Transformation Specialist, Senior, in Infor's Global Professional Services, based in Düsseldorf. I've worked every stage of an ERP transformation: I configured it, I architected it, and now I decide which deals are ready to be signed.

## How I got here

My engineering mindset started with studies in mechanical engineering. I then studied Business and Engineering at THWS in Schweinfurt, specialising in procurement, and did two internships at Schaeffler, in the Regional CEO Office Europe and in product management.

I joined Infor in Düsseldorf in September 2021. As Associate Consultant and then Consultant, I implemented Infor CloudSuite for food & beverage and chemicals customers. As Solution Architect, I took part in pre-sales: I worked with the Solution Consulting and Sales teams to understand customers' requirements and propose the solution stack, which is what the solution proposal, scope and estimates are built on. I then carried that solution into delivery. Since February 2026 I've worked in governance, deciding which services deals are ready to be signed.

## How I work

### One story across every document

I read the scope, estimate, plan and contract side by side until they describe the same commitment. On one complex bid the main documents described different versions of the scope and responsibilities. I documented the contradictions and worked through them with the team, and the submission was amended around one coherent delivery position.

### Ask the hard questions early

I'd rather join a working session while the solution is still being shaped than receive a finished document at the end. Earlier involvement on complex bids means gaps are fixed while the solution and estimate are still being shaped.

### Separate what must be fixed from what needs a decision

Some findings are errors. Others are business choices that someone has to make consciously. Keeping them apart keeps the discussion focused.

### Think from delivery backwards

Having configured and architected these systems, I look at what a promise means on the project, not just whether the document is complete. When a work order used vague language for key prerequisites and possible extra work, I asked the team to state requirements, assumptions and boundaries clearly. The revised version was clear enough for the team to proceed.
